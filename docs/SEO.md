# SEO on settlenotes.com

What is in place, what the owner does by hand once, and the one rule about the legal pages.

## What is in place

- One canonical address per page, all coming from `APP_BASE_URL`. If it is not set at build time the site falls back to `https://settlenotes.com`, so every tag agrees on one origin.
- A title and description on every page. The landing page carries `Settle: your group chat, sorted` and an under 160 character description that says what the bot does. Every other page gets its own title through the `page title | Settle` pattern.
- Open Graph tags (title, description, type, site name, page address, language en_SG) and Twitter card tags on every page, with a real 1200x630 preview image at `/opengraph-image.png`. That picture is generated by `scripts/build-brand.ts` from the same sources as the app icons.
- `/robots.txt`: the landing page is open, `/dashboard`, `/api/`, `/auth/`, `/accept` and `/login` are blocked, and the file points crawlers at the sitemap and the site host.
- `/sitemap.xml`: the landing page always, plus the Terms, Privacy and Refund pages only while their documents are complete. It is built fresh on each request.
- Structured data (JSON-LD) on the landing page: Settle as an organisation and a website, and as a free educational app that runs on Telegram. It states the free plan only, because Pro is not on sale.
- The dashboard, login, accept and auth pages tell search engines not to index them and not to follow their links.
- The browser theme colour is pinned to the brand ink, and phones are told not to auto detect phone numbers or addresses in the copy.

## Submitting to Google Search Console

1. Open Google Search Console in a browser and sign in with any Google account.
2. Add a property and choose the Domain option, entering `settlenotes.com` (not a single page address). A Domain property covers every path and both www forms at once.
3. Choose DNS verification when Google offers the methods. It shows a TXT record value to copy.
4. Open the domain registrar, find the DNS records page, add a TXT record with that value, and save. Changes usually show up within minutes to a few hours.
5. Back in Google Search Console, click verify.
6. Once verified, open the Sitemaps page, enter `https://settlenotes.com/sitemap.xml` and submit. Discovery of the pages rolls in over the following days.

## Submitting to Bing Webmaster Tools

1. Open Bing Webmaster Tools and sign in. The easiest path is importing the site from Google Search Console, which carries the site and the sitemap over with no extra verification.
2. If importing is not wanted, add `https://settlenotes.com` by hand and verify with the DNS option (a CNAME or TXT record at the registrar, the same steps as above).
3. Submit the same sitemap, `https://settlenotes.com/sitemap.xml`. Bing also feeds DuckDuckGo and several other search engines, so this one submission covers more than Bing itself.

## How to check the tags by hand

- View source on a page in a normal browser window and look for the canonical link and the `og:` and `twitter:` meta tags in the head.
- Open `https://settlenotes.com/robots.txt` and `https://settlenotes.com/sitemap.xml` directly in a browser.
- For the structured data, Google's Rich Results Test checks a live address and reports what it recognised.
- For the share preview, Facebook's Sharing Debugger and LinkedIn's Post Inspector show the picture and text a posted link will get. These are third party pages the owner opens at will; nothing on the site depends on them.

## The legal pages rule

The Terms, Privacy and Refund pages stay out of search engines while any blank remains. Their metadata rechecks the filled document on every request: if a marker is present (a missing setting, an unanswered counsel question, an unknown token), the page says noindex and stays out of the sitemap. Once the legal settings are filled in the environment, the pages turn indexable by themselves and join the sitemap on the next request.

## Deliberately not done

- No keyword stuffing: the copy says what the product does, once.
- No ratings, reviews or award claims in the structured data, and no Pro price, because Pro is not on sale.
- No analytics, no ad cookies, no third party scripts for search purposes.
- No hidden text: everything a search engine sees, a reader sees too.
