Settle — the study companion that turns student chaos into a plan, then helps you do the work
Students don’t fail because they can’t do the work. They lose track of it. Deadlines sit in group chats, assignment briefs run 30 pages, slides pile up, and mistakes in practice papers go unnoticed until the exam. I see this daily as a CS undergraduate and as a math and computing tutor, and I want to build the tool my students and I actually need.
Settle is a Telegram bot with a web dashboard. Students dump anything into it: group chat screenshots, assignment PDFs, lecture slides, voice notes, or photos of handwritten math working. Muse Spark reads all of it and builds a single prioritised task list with deadlines. It understands Singlish chats, so “prof say extend to nxt fri liao” updates an existing deadline instead of creating a duplicate task.
Each task then comes with its first step done:
Assignment brief: a checklist of deliverables, submission rules and rubric requirements.
Upcoming test: flashcards and practice questions generated from the student’s own slides.
Photo of math working: Settle pinpoints the exact step where the reasoning went wrong, not just that the answer is wrong. It then generates 2–3 targeted questions on that misconception.
Those mistakes feed a weak-spot tracker, and the tracker schedules practice back into the task list. Learning gaps become tasks like any other, so nothing slips.
It fits “Build for Your World”: it gives students back the mental energy they spend remembering what to do, so they can spend it learning. The 10-day plan is to build the capture and task loop first, then the three starters, then the weak-spot loop. I’ll test it with my own tutoring students.
